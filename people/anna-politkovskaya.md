## Lists

- **CPJ.** The CPJ data API (`https://cpj.org/wp-json/cpj-datamanager/v1/people_list?status=Killed&motiveConfirmed=Confirmed&type=Journalist&start_year=2006&end_year=2006&cc_fips=RS`), fetched on 26 October 2026, returns a row with: name Anna Politkovskaya; organisation Novaya Gazeta; location Russia; status Killed; type of death Murder; date October 7, 2006; type Journalist; motive Confirmed. The filters in that request did not narrow the results (the response mixed years and unconfirmed rows); the row was found on page 1. CPJ's person page, `https://cpj.org/data/people/anna-politkovskaya/`, fetched the same day, says she was 48, was a special correspondent for the Moscow newspaper Novaya Gazeta, and was found dead in her apartment building in Moscow, where, according to Interfax citing police, she had been shot. The same page says she was known for her reporting on human rights abuses in Chechnya.
- **RSF.** RSF's barometer (`https://rsf.org/en/barometer`) says it lists journalists only where it has established that the death was linked to their journalistic work. The barometer rows at `https://rsf.org/en/barometre/update/201?annee_end=2026&annee_start=1995&type%5Btue%5D=tue`, fetched on 26 October 2026, include a row with: first name "Annai"; family name "Politovskaïa"; media Novaya Gazeta; status Killed; date 07/10/2006. No job is given in the row. The spelling is RSF's and is recorded as it appears.
- **IFJ.** Not verified. IFJ's 2006 killed list (`http://ifj.org/assets/docs/036/210/bb56224-fe100d2.pdf`) returned HTTP 503 on 26 October 2026.

CPJ and RSF agree on the date (7 October 2006) and the outlet (Novaya Gazeta). They spell her name differently. This entry uses CPJ's spelling as its title and does not choose between them.

## Work

- **Link.** "Посадили за национальную почву" ("Jailed on ethnic grounds"; our translation, not checked by a native speaker), Novaya Gazeta, rubric "Кавказский узел" ("Caucasian Knot"). The page gives the byline Анна Политковская and says it appeared in issue No. 69 of 11 September 2006. It is in Russian. Served on 26 October 2026 at `https://novayagazeta.ru/articles/2006/09/11/27971-posadili-za-natsionalnuyu-pochvu`. Whether this was the article's address in 2006 is not known.
- **Capture.** An existing Internet Archive capture from 28 March 2022: `http://web.archive.org/web/20220328233521/https://novayagazeta.ru/articles/2006/09/11/27971-posadili-za-natsionalnuyu-pochvu`. Checked on 26 October 2026; it keeps the title, the issue line, the byline and a dateline of 20:00, 10 September 2006.
- **Summary, in our words.** The article is about young Chechen men, most of them former students, serving terrorism sentences after the detentions of 2002 to 2004. Because she was not allowed to contact him, Politkovskaya rebuilds one case from documents: Islam Suskhanov, born in 1984, a former student of the arts faculty at the Chechen pedagogical institute. She sets the references written for him by his school, his faculty and his football coach beside the charges, and lists where the dates in three episodes of the case do not match. She follows his 14-year sentence through prison colonies in Sverdlovsk region, including solitary confinement, two suicide attempts in 2006 and a ban on praying, and records a regional ombudsman's support for a transfer and the prison service's refusal. She quotes a letter from the prisoners' mothers. She ends by looking ahead to about 2017, when this group would be released in their mid-thirties, and says what she fears about that.
